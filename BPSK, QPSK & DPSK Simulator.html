Experiment 10 — BPSK, QPSK & DPSK

Normalized baseband simulation (Es = 1 per symbol, complex AWGN). Coherent BPSK and Gray-coded QPSK assume zero carrier phase; DBPSK and DQPSK use differential encoding with differential detection. A carrier phase offset θ rotates the whole received constellation.

Expected: a coherent receiver with no phase correction loses a factor cos θ in BPSK amplitude (QPSK: cos θ ± sin θ per axis), so BER rises with θ and reaches 50% at 90° (QPSK is already unusable at 45°, where the symbols reach the decision boundary). Differential detection compares each symbol with the previous one, so a constant phase offset cancels and BER stays at its θ = 0 value — at the price of roughly 1 dB (DBPSK) to 2–3 dB (DQPSK) more Eb/N0 because the reference itself is noisy.
